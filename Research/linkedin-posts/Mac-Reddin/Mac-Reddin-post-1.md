[It's a lot easier to be a spreadsheet manager than a human manager.

That's one contributing factor to why a lot of sales leaders still cling to the old predictable revenue model in practice, even if they don't call it that anymore.

It's what they were trained on, and it's very easy to manage.

X inputs lead to Y actions which lead to Z outcomes. 

If only reality was that easy and clean cut, then maybe all your AI tools would actually work as promised huh?

I walked through 5 months worth of sales numbers with a sales leader recently - their data showed that 40% of meetings were coming from cold calls, yet they represented only 15% of closed won revenue.

Meetings sourced from social and referrals? Only 12%.

But that 12% of meetings represented an insane 33% of closed won revenue.

Instead of trying to figure out how to get 20% of meetings from those sources, the sales leaders reaction was "I gotta get my team better at cold calling."

And sure, maybe that's worth investing in too. Maybe thats easier to manage and replicate across your reps.

But when the data stares you right in the face and shows you where you can find quality instead of chasing quantity, you gotta lean in.

The best sellers I know though, they lean far away from the spreadsheet and the input driven model. They lean into the human and relationship model. 

It's a lot harder to manage, a lot harder to replicate across other reps, and a whole lot less 'predictable'.

So it scares sales managers.

Don't be that kind of sales leader.]
